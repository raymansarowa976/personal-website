# Welcome Back
In my last post, I talked about productivity and how I am working to maintain it in my day-to-day. 
While the reflection I went through was productive, I believe this post could be a good opportunity to 
expand on some things. I believe some of the metrics I adopted were misguided, and I also want to discuss 
the difference between motivation and discipline, and which one I find more important. I'll dive into the first 
topic, using it as a chance to evaluate my definitions of success and productivity. Then I'll dive into discipline 
and motivation, examples of them in popular media, and what I think about them. 

I've mostly completed my block lock extension; all that's left is to do a testing round and try getting it on the Chrome Web Store. 
Trying to commit every single day until the app was complete was mostly a good idea, but I realized some problems with it. 
Some days, I'm just too busy with other stuff. Sometimes it's having to spend time with family members or deal with job applications, 
or even unexpected stuff that tends to pop up. All that's left with the project is testing out the current project by myself, noting any bugs, 
and fixing those bugs. After that, I'm not sure what will happen. It might get rejected from the Chrome Web Store, or it might get accepted but not 
attract any users. Whatever happens, it'll be a milestone reached that I've been waiting a long time for. Even if my extension is able to get a few 
downloads, I'll be elated. The reason I'm talking about a project I've been working on is because working on projects made me realize how much I have 
to learn when it comes to software development. This is why I've added a bookshelf section on this website. I want to hold myself accountable by publicly 
tracking which books I'm currently reading, have yet to start, and have already read. And if it isn't clear from my writing, I have not completed any of them 
yet. All the books are on core software development and computer science concepts, so I thought they would improve my knowledge and skills. Hopefully, others who 
view my website might see the books on my bookshelf and find something they like as well. I believe this is the best way to keep yourself consistent with a goal. 
Make that goal public, so you'll feel the social pressure to live up to it. Some people might feel like this puts unneeded pressure on themselves, but I believe that 
unneeded pressure is actually what's needed. Disregarding popular advice is sometimes the best way forward, and sometimes so is just disregarding what's popular in general. 
All this ties into what I want to talk about in my next paragraph.

People always talk about motivation. The motivation industrial complex is a billion-dollar industry. Self-help books are some of the biggest bestsellers of all time. 
Videos about motivation get millions of views, and individuals have built entire careers off of being motivators or displaying themselves as extremely motivated. All of 
this talk about motivation, yet none about discipline. What is the difference between motivation and discipline? Motivation can best be characterized as a feeling. You 
feel amped up, so you do the thing you need to do. Discipline is more of a behaviour, meaning you ignore your feelings and do what you need to do regardless. The thing 
about motivation is, if you don't have it, you just won't do anything. Not to say it can't be great. If you need to study for a test that's happening the next day, you 
might need to get a really strong sense of needing to get stuff done. However, if you had built discipline, which is something you need to spend time developing, you wouldn't 
have been in that situation in the first place. Relating this to my first paragraph, motivation would be grinding out the project in sprints, likely creating buggy software in a 
rush, while discipline would be doing a little bit every single day, at the same time every single day, because I took the time to develop that habit. It's the same thing for studying, 
going to the gym, or pretty much everything else in life. You can even apply it to your personal life and see if it reinforces my point. Looking at examples in popular media, consider 
Naruto. At the beginning of the show, Naruto always losing because he's unable to control his emotions despite being extremly motivated due to the bullying he gets in the hidden leaf village. 
As the show progressing, he meets Rock Lee. Rock Lee has no natural ability, isn't very smart, but has something other Ninju lack. An insane ammount of discipline. He has himself weighed down 
with heavy weights while fighting, and is only allowed to take them off when he is pushed to his absolute limit. Naruto sees that there is much more holding Rock Lee back than even himself, yet 
Rock Lee never gives up. We also see this in the several training arcs Naruto goes through with his various Sensis. Anyways, this is all I have to write on this, so I'll now move to the conclusion.
